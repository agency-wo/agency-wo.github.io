An assistant answers your summer visitor from somebody else's page.

It will not say it does not know. It names three places, and one of them might be you.

By Henri Sila · 3 September 2026

The question changes language and the answer does not wait

Out of season somebody here asks in Italian about something practical. In July somebody in Munich or Manchester asks a similar thing in German or English, weeks before arriving. Both get an answer.

What differs is where the answer was built from. When your own pages say nothing in that language, an assistant assembles it out of whatever else mentions the lake, and that is usually a booking platform.

Ten results became three names

A results page handed a visitor ten options and let them scroll. An assistant gives two or three and stops. Around a lake with hundreds of places to eat and sleep, that filter is far harder than any ranking was.

And it issues no report. Nobody informs you that you were left out of an answer, which is why most owners here have no idea any of this is happening.

Being listed on a platform is not being named

When the answer gives your town and then points at a booking site, the visitor books through the booking site and you pay the commission you were trying to avoid. You appear inside somebody else's page instead of as a business with one of your own.

The remedy is the boring one. Pages in the languages your season actually speaks, saying what you offer, carrying an address and a number that gets answered.

The season puts the work in the wrong month

Somebody planning a July week on the lake is asking in March. Whatever a machine can read about you in March is what it repeats. By the time you are busy the answers have already settled.

So the writing belongs in the empty months, which are exactly the months when paying for writing feels hardest to justify.

What we would not promise here

We cannot place you in an answer on request. Nobody can, and whoever says otherwise is selling you the acronym. What we can do is read what an assistant says about the lake today and show you which pages its sentences came from.

Questions worth asking

Does an assistant answer in the language of the question?

It answers in the language you used, whether or not anything on your own site is written that way. That is this whole article in one sentence.

Do we need German pages as well as English?

Only if German visitors are a real share of your season. One more language is one more to keep current, and a page left to rot does more harm than a missing one.

Can we check this ourselves?

Yes, in five minutes. Ask an assistant in German for somewhere to eat or stay on the lake and read the names it hands back. Repeat it a few times, because they move.

Does the map listing still matter for this?

Yes. The details on it are among the few facts about you that agree everywhere, and agreement is most of what these systems check.

Is this instead of the ordinary search work?

No, and the Como piece on ordinary search is still where to begin. This is the same site read by a different reader.
