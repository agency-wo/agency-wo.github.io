The hard part is not the shop. It is getting paid.

Written after building shops for clients who all hit the same wall in the same order.

By Henri Sila · 22 August 2026

Start with the part everybody skips

How does the money reach you. Card payment online is the question that decides the shape of everything else, and it is worth answering before a single product page exists.

Businesses build the shop first, discover the answer, and rebuild. That order is the most common and the most expensive.

The version that works immediately

Product pages with real photographs, honest prices, and a WhatsApp button. No cart, no checkout, no card processing.

It is not a compromise, it is how a great deal of trade here actually happens. People want to ask a question before they buy, and a conversation converts better than a form.

Delivery decides your prices

Work out what it costs to send one item, to a city, and to a village, before you publish a price. Free delivery that you have not costed is a discount you did not choose to give.

Say the cost on the page. Delivery discovered at the last step is the commonest reason a full cart is abandoned.

Returns, said out loud

Write what happens if it does not fit or does not work, in one short paragraph, and put it where people can see it before they buy.

Nobody enjoys writing it. Not having it is read as an answer anyway, and not a good one.

Then the boring things that decide it

Photographs of the actual item. Sizes and materials written out. Stock that is true today. A phone number that gets answered.

None of that is a platform decision, and all of it separates the shops that sell from the shops that exist.

Questions worth asking

Do I need a proper e-commerce platform?

Not to begin with. If you sell fewer than a few dozen items and talk to customers anyway, product pages and a WhatsApp button will carry you a long way, and you will learn what to build from real orders.

Can I just sell on Instagram?

You can, and many do, but you cannot be found there by somebody searching for the product. It is a good second channel and a bad only one, because nothing you post is readable by a search engine.

What about selling abroad?

Then payments and shipping change completely and the answer stops being local. It is worth doing properly rather than bolting onto a domestic shop, and it usually needs a conversation before a build.

How many products before it is worth a real shop?

When you cannot keep stock accurate by hand, or when writing back to every buyer takes longer than the order is worth. Both are signals from the work rather than from a number somebody made up.

Is cash on delivery still normal here?

Yes, and designing as though it were not is how a shop ends up with abandoned carts it does not understand. Offer it, price it honestly, and say so on the page.
