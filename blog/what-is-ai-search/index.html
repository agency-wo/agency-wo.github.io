minarank studio / Blog / What AI search actually is

Somebody asks a question and gets three names, not ten links.

Written for somebody who has heard the phrase and reasonably suspects it is mostly noise.

By Henri Sila · 22 August 2026

The change in one sentence

A search engine hands you a list and lets you choose. An assistant reads the list for you and answers with two or three names.

Everything else follows from that. Position eleven used to mean a trickle of visitors; in an answer that names three businesses it means nothing at all.

Where the answers come from

Text that can be read and checked. Pages that state plainly what a business is, where it is and what it sells, plus what other people have written about it somewhere else.

Which is why a business living entirely inside a social account is invisible here: there is nothing an assistant can read and nothing it can corroborate.

Why the boring things matter more than the clever ones

Consistent details across the web, an address written the same way everywhere, real answers to real questions, and reviews somebody else wrote. None of it is a trick and all of it is checkable.

That is the uncomfortable part for the industry: what works is mostly the same unglamorous work that has always worked.

What nobody can promise

That an assistant will name you. There is no submission form, no index to be listed in, and the answers change between one asking and the next.

Anybody guaranteeing a mention is selling certainty that does not exist, and the honest version of the offer is to make you the obvious thing to name and accept that the rest is not ours to decide.

Whether it matters yet for you

It depends who buys from you. Trades where people ask around are affected sooner; a shop people walk past is affected later.

The useful thing is that the work overlaps almost entirely with ordinary search, so nobody has to bet on a timeline to justify doing it.

Questions worth asking

Is this a rebrand of ordinary SEO?

Mostly it is the same foundations, and the difference is real but narrow: being one of three named rather than one of ten listed raises the cost of being nearly good enough.

Do I need to do anything different?

Very little, and that is the honest answer even though it sells nothing. Write plainly, keep your details consistent everywhere, answer real questions, and collect reviews.

Can I stop assistants using my content?

You can ask them not to, and some respect it. For a small business wanting customers it is usually the wrong instinct: being unreadable is the same as being unmentioned.

How would I know if it is working?

By asking, repeatedly, and writing down what comes back. There is no dashboard. It is closer to checking a shelf in a shop than reading a report, and anybody showing you a precise score invented it.

Is it worth paying for yet?

As a separate service, for most small businesses, not yet. As a reason to do the ordinary work properly, yes, because that work pays either way and this is one more reason for it.
